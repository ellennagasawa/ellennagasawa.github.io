Teaching Philosophy

I understand language as more than a system of grammar and vocabulary. Language is a social practice through which individuals construct meaning, express identities, and participate in culturally and historically situated communities. My goal as an educator is to help students develop the linguistic, intercultural, and critical literacy needed to communicate ethically, confidently, and purposefully in diverse contexts.

I view language proficiency as the ability to use language effectively across different social situations. Language education should therefore prepare students not only to understand linguistic structures, but also to participate thoughtfully in academic, professional, cultural, and civic communities. This perspective informs my commitment to equitable learning opportunities, critical citizenship, and meaningful communication.

Ultimately, I believe that language education must extend beyond the classroom. My role is to empower students to use language with purpose and agency, build relationships across borders, and reflect critically on how language shapes their experiences and the world around them.

Teaching Approach

Student-centered and contextualized learning

My courses are student-centered, task-based, and grounded in contextualized language use. I treat reading, writing, listening, and speaking as interconnected social practices rather than isolated skills. Students work with authentic communicative situations through discussions, collaborative presentations, genre-based writing, videos, role-plays, and longer-term projects.

Activities are carefully scaffolded so that students can build confidence while progressively expanding their linguistic and intercultural abilities. Students are encouraged to draw on their existing linguistic knowledge, experiences, interests, and professional goals as resources for learning.

Classroom community

I view the classroom as a community of practice built on trust, respect, collaboration, and student agency. I strive to create an environment in which students feel comfortable experimenting with language, asking questions, taking intellectual risks, and learning from one another.

Authentic materials—including literature, songs, news, social media, videos, menus, advertisements, and cultural artifacts—connect language learning with real social and cultural practices. These materials help students understand not only how Portuguese is structured, but also how it is used to create meaning across Portuguese-speaking communities.

Assessment and feedback

My approach to assessment is process-oriented, transparent, and holistic. I evaluate students’ development from their individual starting points and provide opportunities for improvement through instructor feedback, peer review, self-assessment, and multiple drafts.

Reflective journals, analytical discussions, debates, presentations, creative projects, and performance-based tasks allow students to demonstrate both linguistic development and critical engagement with course content. Feedback is treated as part of the learning process rather than simply as justification for a final grade.

Educational technology

I use educational technology to expand opportunities for interaction, practice, reflection, and feedback. My courses incorporate Canvas, interactive presentations, collaborative writing spaces, video and audio production, digital annotation, and carefully selected AI-supported tools. Technology is used purposefully to support learning objectives rather than as an end in itself.

Curriculum and Course Design

I have designed more than ten online, hybrid, and in-person courses using platforms such as Canvas and Moodle. My curriculum work connects clearly defined learning outcomes with authentic communicative tasks, multimodal instructional materials, formative feedback, and performance-based assessment.

Major course-design projects include Portuguese Elements I and II at Johns Hopkins University, Portuguese as an Additional Language, English for Public Relations, academic and professional writing, Celpe-Bras preparation, and English for professional and academic contexts.

Portuguese Elements I and II

Portuguese Elements I and II are accelerated, fully online courses designed to introduce students to foundational Portuguese while developing intercultural knowledge of Portuguese-speaking communities. The courses integrate interactive modules, authentic texts, embedded audio and video, role-play tasks, cultural inquiry, writing, listening, and speaking activities.

Because the courses are asynchronous, their design emphasizes clear navigation, coherent instructions, responsive instructor support, and frequent opportunities for students to apply language in realistic situations. Activities such as interpreting menus, making reservations, navigating cities, and describing personal preferences connect linguistic development with meaningful communication.

Teaching materials and projects

My teaching materials include modular Canvas lessons, interactive presentations, culturally grounded reading and listening activities, task-based assessments, creative writing projects, speaking tasks, and materials for Celpe-Bras preparation. I also design projects that connect literature, social history, culture, and language development.

Johns Hopkins University Course Evaluations

Peer Observation of Teaching

A formal peer observation conducted in Fall 2024 by the Johns Hopkins University Spanish and Portuguese Language Program evaluated my Intermediate Portuguese II and Advanced Portuguese I classes as Very Good.

The observation highlighted the organization and execution of the lesson, the connection between course objectives and real-world language use, the effective integration of classroom technology and instructional resources, and the balance of collaborative, independent, and class-wide learning activities.

The observer also emphasized the respectful and encouraging classroom climate, my use of timely and supportive corrective feedback, and my command of Portuguese vocabulary, grammar, culture, and foreign-language teaching methodology.

“Your teaching was highly effective, demonstrating mastery in both content knowledge and pedagogical skills. You created a dynamic learning environment where students were encouraged to think critically, collaborate, and engage with the material in meaningful ways.”

Flavia A.C., peer classroom observation, Johns Hopkins University, Fall 2024

The observation also recommended using warm-up activities more consistently throughout the semester. I incorporated this recommendation into my reflective teaching plan as part of my continuing course-development process.

Across five Johns Hopkins University course sections evaluated between Fall 2024 and Spring 2026, 29 of 30 enrolled students completed course evaluations, representing an overall survey response rate of 96.7%.

Twenty-eight students submitted a rating of my teaching effectiveness. Twenty-six selected Excellent and two selected Good, producing a response-weighted mean of 4.93 out of 5.

Across the five sections, the response-weighted means were 4.78 out of 5 for overall course quality, 4.56 for intellectual challenge, and 4.85 for the usefulness of feedback.

Reflective Use of Student Feedback

I use student evaluations as a source of evidence for continuous course improvement. I look for patterns across quantitative results, open-ended comments, student performance, and my own classroom observations rather than responding to comments in isolation.

Recent feedback has informed changes such as providing earlier notice of co-curricular events, clarifying assignment deadlines and expectations, streamlining digital platforms, and incorporating additional grammar review and differentiated language practice.

This reflective process allows me to preserve the rigor, authenticity, and cultural depth of my courses while responding thoughtfully to students’ learning needs and experiences.

My teaching extends to the preparation and mentoring of language educators. I have taught courses and workshops in curriculum and materials design, language assessment, programs for teaching Portuguese as an Additional Language, and preparation for the Celpe-Bras examination.

I have also mentored future educators, contributed to the development of teacher-training materials, and participated in national language-testing initiatives. My teaching continues to develop through collaboration with students and colleagues, engagement with applied linguistics research, and reflection on evidence from classroom practice.
